# Wraeclast run: design v10

The owner wants an actually good game, not mobile slop: the pull of Slay the Spire and Hades, simple rules with real depth.
Something must grow over a run, a build you chase, and a player who knows only the art must not be able to win.
It can be long, but never tedious, and every system must be about knowing items; if it works the same without the trivia, it is off the plot.

## Design A: Name it, wear it, break the wall
### 1. The game in two sentences
Wraeclast run is three acts of five fights toward the Seed of Corruption, where every attack is naming an unidentified unique from one tell (its art, its base, a shared mod line or its lore), and any unique you name can be worn in PoE2's slots. The Seed shows three build lines from the first step; only worn pieces that carry them make your hits bigger, and each act's wall takes no other hit, so the run is a hunt for the handful of uniques that carry them.
### 2. The rules
1. A run is three acts of five fights at real places of Wraeclast. Each act ends at a wall: a root of the Seed of Corruption (working name) after acts 1 and 2, the Seed itself on The Cathedral Rooftop after act 3.
2. From the first step the Seed shows three mod lines, such as Rarity of Items found • Chaos Resistance • Armour and Evasion. The first counts from act 1, the second from act 2, the third from act 3.
3. A fight is a foe with 2 to 7 life and a hand of three unidentified uniques, face up. One of them always carries a line that counts now.
4. Each card shows one tell, set by the place. Known: the art. Obscure: the base, or a line found on 3 to 15 uniques (never its own famous line). Forgotten: its lore with the name words blacked out.
5. Tap a card, then its name from four real uniques that look alike but don't fit the tell. There is no clock.
6. Right: the Fontin tooltip opens, and the foe takes 1 hit plus 1 for each counted line on the gear you wear. The screen always shows that number.
7. Wrong: no hit. The card turns to show what it was, and that unique goes to the Seed.
8. Then the foe strikes: 1 in act 1, 2 in act 2, 3 in act 3. It falls when its life runs out, and any card not yet named is gone. If the hand runs out first, three more come.
9. After a win, wear one unique you named there, or none. You wear five pieces at most, one per PoE2 slot (two rings; a two-hander fills both hands), so a new piece often replaces one. Charms, flasks, jewels, relics and tablets can be named but never worn.
10. Then pick the next place from two. Each shows its depth and one unique waiting there, by its tell.
11. You have 25 life. Only beating a wall heals you: it refills your life and lets you wear one unique you named there.
12. Walls have 9, 16 and 24 life, show no art, and take only the counted hits, never the 1. Every unique you missed waits in the Seed's hand, now showing its rarest line.
13. Uniques drop by required level: up to 25 in act 1, up to 50 in act 2, any in act 3.
14. At 0 life the run ends. A win opens the next Waystone tier and lets a piece you wore start a later run; you pick it once you see that run's Seed.
### 3. A run, start to end
- Clearfell Encampment: 25 life, no gear. The Seed reads Rarity of Items found • Chaos Resistance • Armour and Evasion. The root at Ogham Manor waits with 9 life, and only Rarity can wound it.
- The Red Vale (Known): a foe with 2 life. You know Goldrim's art and that it carries Rarity; you name it, its Rarity line glows, 1 hit. A second art you know fells the foe. You wear Goldrim: a right name now hits 2.
- The fork: Freythorn (Known) shows Visage of Ayah, another Rarity helmet, and you have one head. Maraketh (Obscure) shows "Layered Gauntlets": Aurseize, alone on that base, with Rarity now and Armour and Evasion in act 3. Seven of act 1's ten Rarity uniques are helmets, so you take the harder road. You hit 3.
- Kaom's Stronghold (Forgotten): the foe has 3 life, so one right name ends it, and you must name the card you want, not the one you are surest of. Lore of a tangled, unknown future is Gamblesprint, Rarity boots. You hit 4.
- Ogham Manor: the root shows bases, lines and lore, never art, and takes 3 a name. One miss, three right names, and it falls. Life back to 25.
- Act 2, and Chaos Resistance counts. A road card at The Lunaris Temple shows lore of a queen banished from Keth: Serpent's Egg, Rarity and Chaos Resistance, so it counts twice. Then Original Sin. Six counted lines: a right name hits 7.
- The near miss: the root at Heart of the Tribe (16 life, strikes 2) takes 6 a name; you arrive on 8. Lore of the best-loved hand on a Brinerot crew, the one who never works. Pirates, so you guess Cat O' Nine Tails. Wrong: it's The Black Cat, a charm, gone to the Seed. Three right names in a row, and the root falls with you on 2.
- Act 3: Armour and Evasion counts, so Aurseize counts twice. Twilight Strand (Known) shows Lightning Coil; Oriath Harbour (Obscure) shows "Knight Armour". Widow's Reign sits there with Chaos Resistance and Armour and Evasion, two counted lines to one. It replaces Original Sin. A right name hits 9.
- The Cathedral Rooftop: the Seed (24 life, strikes 3) takes 8 a name. "Lightning Damage of Enemies Hitting you is Unlucky during effect": you read it when The Black Cat beat you. "Plate Gauntlets" is Valako's Vice. Then lore you know, and the Seed falls with you on 7. Waystone II opens, and Serpent's Egg can start your next run.
### 4. What grows over a run, and how two runs' builds differ
- Gear grows from none to five pieces and about eight counted lines: a right name goes from 1 hit to about 9, a wall from untouchable to three names. Each act adds a line, so an early piece can wake up later, as Aurseize did.
- The Seed draws its three from 22 lines, each on 10 to 30 wearable uniques across five or more slots (Rarity, Chaos Resistance, Cold Resistance, Energy Shield, Life Regeneration, Stun Threshold and the like): about 6,000 Seeds. Life (59 uniques), Mana (54) and the attributes (44 to 48) never count, nor do lines stuck in one or two slots. Six uniques carry two of a run's lines; they are the jackpots, like Hades' duo boons, and with five slots every swap is a trade.
- A Seed of Cold Resistance, Energy Shield and Life Regeneration needs none of this run's pieces. In the model in section 5, two winning runs share even one unique only 21% of the time, and 258 different uniques were worn at a win.
### 5. Why knowing items deeply wins
- Each hand asks three things the card never answers: which card carries a counted line, can I name it, and is it worth a strike if I can't. One right name ends 73% of a veteran's fights, so the sure card often kills and leaves the piece you wanted behind.
- A veteran reads "Layered Gauntlets" as Aurseize and knows it pays twice, takes Widow's Reign over Lightning Coil, grabs Erian's Cobble on sight (9 of its 15 lines among the 22), and leaves Headhunter (Life, Strength, Dexterity and its own famous line).
- Each tell needs its own knowledge. 323 of the 445 uniques are alone on their base. An Obscure line is on 3 to 15 uniques and the decoys never carry it, so only memory of the four names decides. Lore needs lore.
- Art opens the door and no more: Known places give you pieces, but walls show no art and take only counted hits.
- A rough model, 4,000 runs per player: a veteran wins 79%; one who can't tell what a unique carries until its tooltip opens, 66%; one who knows bases and famous lines but half the rest, 60%; a main-game regular passes the first root 79% of the time and wins 7%; perfect art with guesses elsewhere, 0.8%; a veteran who never wears anything, never.
### 6. The 20th run against the 1st
- Run 1: you know the art and a few dozen names. Known places hand you pieces, and glowing tooltip lines show what counts. You fall at the second root on a lore card; the end screen shows that unique with the Seed's lines marked, and what naming it would have done: 6 hits against a root with 4 left.
- Run 20: you know most bases, the build lines of a few hundred uniques and the famous lore. You plan slots from the Seed at step one, follow road cards to two-line pieces, and take the unsure card only when your life can afford a strike.
- Waystone tiers stack one rule each. II: six names, not four. III: Obscure shows Life and attribute lines with numbers, so only ranges tell Lightning Coil's "+(80-100) to maximum Life" from Bones of Ullr's "+(20-40)". V: art mirrored and grey. VII: no hand promises a carrier. X: the Seed shows only lore. The top is for the few, as Ascension 20 is in Slay the Spire (7.3% of owners).
### 7. Why a long run never drags
- A fight takes about 30 seconds: pick, name, the tooltip glows, hits count down the foe, then you wear a piece and watch the next number rise.
- The question changes every act (one counted line, then two, then three), and drops widen: 167 wearable uniques to level 25, 120 more to 50, then all 400. The next wall is on screen all act, so every pick asks: does this break it?
- No shop, gold, supplies, map walk or board; every action is one tap on a 375 px screen. 15 fights and 3 walls, about 40 names, 12 to 15 minutes. A fourth act comes once this loop proves fun.
### 8. What it keeps from the earlier prototypes
- Kept: the Seed on The Cathedral Rooftop, real places, Known, Obscure and Forgotten, real look-alike names, the card look, the Fontin tooltip as the payoff, the codex. From v10: naming as the attack, a build of real uniques in PoE2's slots, misses returning at the Seed, the wall on screen from step one, the end screen, the range tier.
- It is not v7's "wear 3 uniques" pitch, a side screen where you picked three blind and a shared word powered one duel. Here wearing is the whole loop: you earn each piece by naming it, its lines are on its tooltip, and the walls say which lines matter.
- Dropped: the syllable board, supplies, gold, shops, towns, boons, the open map, corruption, Pledges, stars, and any healing outside the walls.
- Lessons kept: nothing can be skipped, every road has a price, a pick changes the very next fight, naming is one tap.
- Not solved: a unique's own famous line, like Headhunter's, can never count, since no other unique carries it. It lives in the tooltip and the Seed's returning cards, not the build.

## Design B: Unidentified
### 1. The game in two sentences
A deckbuilding run across Wraeclast where each fight is one unidentified unique: its dark mod lines are its life and its attack, and you light them by calling words your cards really grant. Every boss is named on the map from the first step, so you read the map's bases as a shopping list and build the deck that answers them, with your own life as the only clock.
### 2. The rules
1. You start at Clearfell Encampment with 40 life and your class's six uniques as cards (Warrior, Ranger or Witch). Life carries from fight to fight.
2. From step one the map names each act's boss (two uniques the Seed has taken) and the Seed's three hearts on The Cathedral Rooftop.
3. Every other place shows only the base of the unique it holds; an elite also shows the rule-changer it guards. Move to any place next to one you've passed.
4. A fight is one unidentified unique, shown as PoE shows one: art, base, base stats, implicit. Its mod lines are dark.
5. A line holds a word if it grants or adds it. The words are PoE's own 13: Fire, Cold, Lightning, Chaos, Physical, Attack, Spell, Minion, Armour, Evasion, Energy Shield, Life, Mana. Elemental means all three elements. Reduced, less and cannot lines hold none.
6. Each turn you draw 5 and get 3 energy. Play a card for 1 and call one of its words, implicit included. Every dark line holding that word lights.
7. A ring, amulet, belt or quiver makes the next card you play free if the two share a damage type, Attack, Spell or Minion.
8. When you end your turn, every dark line hits you: 1 life in act 1, 2 in act 2, 3 in act 3.
9. When every line holding a word is lit, the rest light too: it's identified and the fight ends. Keep it as a card or pass.
10. Or name it for 1 energy, from six real look-alikes. Right: it's yours, and the fight ends after this turn's hits. Wrong: it can't be yours.
11. You may leave a plain fight or an elite at the end of any turn, after its hits, with nothing.
12. An elite hits twice per line. Identify it or name it right and its rule-changer is yours too, doing its real thing all run: Thrillsteel's Onslaught is a fourth energy on each fight's first turn; Kalandra's Touch reflects your rings, so each frees the next two cards.
13. A boss can't be named or left. Identify all its uniques and its town is freed: heal 20 (to at most 40), cut a card, keep one of its uniques. Break the Seed and the run is won.
14. At 0 life the run ends. The end screen names the words your deck lacked that hit you most, and where on your map they were.
### 3. A run
- Clearfell Encampment: a Ranger holds Quill Rain, Tyranny's Grip, Foxshade, Northpaw, Briarpatch and The Lethal Draw (Attack, Physical, Evasion, Life). The map names act 1's boss at Ziggurat Encampment, Yoke of Suffering with Lycosidae, and the Seed's hearts: Corona of the Red Sun, Couture of Crimson, Death Articulated.
- She reads it as a list. The Yoke's three lines are all elemental, so one Fire, Cold or Lightning call opens it. Lycosidae wants Armour and Chaos. A Fire Quiver is always Blackgleam; a Braced Tower Shield is always Doomgate (Armour, Chaos). An Amethyst Ring holds Chaos in its implicit; a Two-Stone Ring holds two elements.
- The Red Vale: a Fire Quiver, five dark lines. Quill Rain calls Attack and two light. She holds no Mana, so she names it, Blackgleam, and takes the three still dark: 37.
- Ogham Manor: nothing she holds lights Doomgate, so she names it at once and takes five: 32. Lycosidae needs both its words and one card calls one, so she turns to an Amethyst Ring at Pools of Khatal. It's Ming's Heart: Doomgate calls Chaos, identified without a hit.
- Temple of Kopec, an elite: Periphery on its Heartwood Shortbow, guarding Thrillsteel. Fire and Attack light three lines; with no Cold or Lightning she names it and takes two lines at double: 28.
- Ziggurat Encampment: Blackgleam calls Fire and the Yoke breaks. Ming's Heart calls Chaos and frees Doomgate, which calls Armour; Lycosidae breaks. No hits. She heals to 40, cuts Foxshade and keeps the Yoke, an amulet that frees her Fire cards.
- Act 2's boss is The Raven's Flock with Ironbound. The Flock's only words are two Minion lines, and she has none. A Pearl Ring at The Fellshrine Ruins might be Heartbound Loop; a Spiked Club at Holten is always Trenchtimbre.
- It nearly fails. The ring is Snakepit: seven lines, Spell or blank, 14 life in one turn. Trenchtimbre costs 2 more. At The Forest Encampment Ironbound falls on turn one, but Trenchtimbre sits at the bottom of her deck while the Flock hits for 10 a turn. On 4 life it comes, calls Minion, and the Flock breaks. She heals to 24, keeps Ironbound, cuts Tyranny's Grip.
- Act 3 hits for three a line; she reaches the Rooftop on 9, and the Seed has fifteen dark lines. The Yoke calls Fire and frees Blackgleam, which calls Attack and frees Ironbound. Ironbound's Armour lights the first line of Couture and of Death Articulated. Briarpatch's Life lights four lines across two hearts. Doomgate calls the last word, Chaos, and the Seed breaks.
### 4. What grows over a run, and how two runs differ
- The deck grows from six cards and four words to a dozen or more, holding the words the named bosses ask for. Links take a turn from three plays to five. Rule-changers bend the rules for good, and each boss leaves one of its uniques. A fight that cost five hits in act 1 costs none by act 3, while each line left dark hurts three times as much.
- A Witch starts on The Dark Defiler (Mana, Chaos, Minion) and Lifesprig (Mana, Spell, Life), so the Pearl Ring that nearly killed the Ranger is sure for her: Snakepit, Evergrasping Ring and Heartbound Loop all match. She hunts an Engraved Focus (always Carrion Call) and a Linen Belt (one of two is Umbilicus Immortalis). With Kalandra's Touch, Heartbound Loop frees Carrion Call and Umbilicus Immortalis, which frees The Dark Defiler: four cards for one energy.
### 5. Why knowing items deeply wins
- The map: 296 of the 400 gear uniques (74%) are alone on their base, so a base is a promise of words. A veteran routes toward the boss's words before the first fight; a newcomer sees a Braced Tower Shield and learns nothing.
- The calls: an enemy holds about 3 of the 13 words. Blind, the best guess (Life) lights something on 44% of enemies, most words on about 25%, Minion on 3%. Every miss costs energy, and every dark line hits.
- The hand: one Armour call lights the first line of both Couture and Death Articulated, and Blackgleam must call Attack since the Yoke has Fire. Grants-only keeps this about what items do: Tyranny's Grip's "reduced Attack Speed" holds no word; its increased Physical Damage does.
- Art alone loses: art names Blackgleam but not which calls light it, and naming ends a fight only after every dark line hits. Paper sims of an act 1 plain fight, hits taken: veteran 3.1 with the start deck, 1.8 with twelve cards; art only 3.6 and 3.7; blind 9.4 and 6.8. Act 3 bosses can't be named, so art alone bleeds out before the Seed.
### 6. The 20th run against the 1st
- Run 1: she knows the words but few uniques. She calls Life because it often lights, names what she recognises, and bleeds out to The Raven's Flock. The end screen says: no Minion in your deck; the Flock hit you for 20; the Spiked Club at Holten was Trenchtimbre. The codex keeps every line she lit, in the item's own tooltip.
- Run 20: she routes each act from the map, knows a Two-Stone Ring answers any Yoke, prices a Pearl Ring as one of three, passes cards that thin her words, and names only when sure. Waystone tiers strip deeper layers: 1, bosses shown by base only; 2, no art, where two dark lines on an Amethyst Ring can only be Original Sin; 3, her own cards show name and art, not lines; 4, the enemy's implicit goes dark; 5, names from ten look-alikes.
### 7. Why a long run never drags
- Four or five fights an act, the boss included, each one to three turns, and every one chosen: a word for the boss, a rule-changer, or greed you priced. Forks differ in kind and in what their bases promise.
- A fight you can read ends in one turn with no hits, so fights get shorter as the deck grows while each dark line hits harder: late acts are fast and tense. Between fights there is only the map.
- On a 375 px phone the tooltip sits on top and five cards below show their word chips. Tap a chip to call; tap a card to read its tooltip. A boss's uniques sit in tabs.
### 8. What it keeps from the earlier prototypes
- From v5: the open region of real places, Clearfell Encampment to The Cathedral Rooftop. From the live game: ItemDetails in Fontin as the enemy, real look-alikes for naming, the codex.
- From v10: a deck of real uniques, the named boss read by base, elites with real rule-changers, life that carries, the end screen, Waystone tiers. From the proxy: the 13 words and grants-only rule, dark lines as life and attack, the named Seed, bosses from all 400 gear uniques. Changed: one word per play, links free the next card, a right early name still takes this turn's hits.
- Still cut: the syllable board, gold, shops, supplies, boons, Pledges, timers, and v10's 37-word legend and life sums.
- Not solved: apart from the rule-changers, a card is its words and its slot, so Quill Rain plays like any one-word Attack card; giving all 400 uniques their own effects means hand-making them.

## Pick
Design A, Name it, wear it, break the wall. Its rules fit on one screen and every one is about items: the piece you name is the piece you wear, the Seed decides which tooltip lines matter, and walls that show no art and take only counted hits make art alone lose.
Its weakest point is the moment to moment act: it is still tapping a name from four, which a playtest already found no fun on its own. The design bets that choosing which card to risk, the glowing lines and the rising hit carry it, and only a phone playtest will tell; Design B grows a richer build but asks for far more reading per turn.

## Questions for the owner
1. How close should a good run feel at the walls?
   - (recommended) On the edge: most wins arrive at a wall low on life, like the near miss at Heart of the Tribe.
   - Comfortable: a strong build walks through, and the edge comes from higher Waystone tiers.
   - Brutal: even a good build loses about half its runs at the Seed.
2. When a right name lands, what should the moment feel like?
   - (recommended) The tooltip opens, each counted line glows in turn, and each glow strikes the foe.
   - One heavy blow showing the total, then straight to the next card.
   - Quick and quiet: a short flash and the next card, to keep the pace up.
3. How long should the first playable run be?
   - (recommended) Three acts, 12 to 15 minutes, as written.
   - Two acts, about 8 minutes, to test the loop sooner.
   - Four acts, about 20 minutes, so the build has longer to grow.
