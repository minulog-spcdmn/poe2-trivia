# Wraeclast run: experts and losses

Two design drafts for the plan's known weak spots. Every rule below keeps the firm decisions: no timer, the syllable Inscription from 4 to 10 names, every submit counts, life plus a heal-over-time flask refilled only at Wells, towns heal 20, gold spent only between items, win or lose, 5 tiers, PoE-themed names, item data made by script, no leaderboards or shared seeds.

Numbers marked *measured* come from the repo's data (scripts in `research/scripts/`: `stats.py`, `rim.py`, `mods.py`, run read-only over `src/data/` and `public/items/`).

---

## 1. Experts: a tier ladder that takes information away

### What an expert leans on today

An expert has four sources before spending anything:

1. **The board.** With 4 names, the syllables are in effect a 4-option list. Name look-alikes are already on it at tier 1.
2. **The free clue.** One patch (Plain, Expedition, Ultimatum, Necropolis), 2 patches (Blight), one mod line (Ritual), flavor text (Incursion), base type (Essence).
3. **Miss feedback.** A wrong real name is struck and its own syllables grey out, so a guess that fails still narrows the board.
4. **The free clue on every item.** All 3 items in a zone open with it.

How decisive the free clue is, *measured*:

- **A free patch.** The Veil is 4 x 4. The 4 centre squares are 80% opaque on average and show 3.4x the edge detail of the 12 rim squares (13.1 against 3.9). A centre patch often names the item for someone who knows the art.
- **A free mod line.** A random explicit mod line, with numbers ignored, belongs to that item alone 48% of the time. So a Ritual zone's free clue settles about half its items outright for a player who knows mods.
- **A base type.** It is the only unique of its base in its category for 323 of 445 uniques (73%). The plan already handles this with Essence's 0.5x.

Tiers 2 to 5 in the plan add names, raise prices and cut life. None of that touches the four sources above. The ladder below takes them away one per tier, in that order.

### The ladder

Each tier keeps the ones below it. Each rule is a single line on the tier card.

| Tier | Name | Rule (exact card text) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Waystone I** | The rules as written. |
| 2 | **Twinned** | The wrong names are the items drawn most like the answer. |
| 3 | **Guttering** | Free clues show the least: rim patches, the most common mod line. |
| 4 | **Unmarked** | A miss strikes only your build; no name or syllable greys out. |
| 5 | **Lightless** | Only a zone's first item has its free clue. |

This replaces the plan's tier rules: +25% and +50% prices, 80 life with a 1-charge flask, +2 names with mirrored patches, and grayscale with a 4-item boss. Mirrored and grayscale stay as the **Mirrored** boss rule, so no tier makes that boss pointless. If a 5th tier still feels light in playtests, the boss's 4th item can come back as the only non-information rule. I'd try without it first.

#### Tier 2: Twinned

- **Rule.** The board's wrong names are taken from the answer's art look-alikes in `looks.json` (same group, highest score first). The board still shows look-alike *names* where the art list runs short.
- **Data, *measured*.** Every one of the 501 items has at least 3 art look-alikes in its own category, so a 4-name board can always be filled. 94% have 7 and 86% have 9, so boss boards fall back to name look-alikes for about 1 item in 7. `looks.json` already exists and is generated by `npm run looks`. Delve uses the same table from depth 49.
- **Why it hits experts.** One patch of a gold ring only names the item when the other ring names on the board look nothing like it. With look-alikes on the board, that patch fits several names, so a confident expert buys a second Flare or a Scroll.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The question is still "which of these is it". The expert who knows the fine details of each piece of art (a gem's colour, a hilt's shape) still wins with fewer supplies. The rule removes the shortcut of "it's the only ring that's round", not the knowledge.

#### Tier 3: Guttering

- **Rule.** A free patch is always one of the 12 rim squares, never one of the 4 centre squares, and never a square under 5% opaque. A free mod line (Ritual's) is the item's most common line, measured over all 501 items. Flares and Scrolls work as before: you still choose your patches, and a Scroll still shows a random line.
- **Data, *measured*.** Rim squares show 3.9 units of edge detail against 13.1 in the centre. 22% of rim squares are almost empty, which is why empty ones are excluded. Using the most common mod line drops "the free line names the item outright" from 48% to 17%. On a median item, that most common line is shared with 29 other items.
- **Automation.**
  - The patch-cutting step on the host already reads the alpha channel, so finding the rim squares needs no new stored data.
  - `make-clues.mjs` adds a `shared` count per mod line (lines matched with their numbers masked).
  - Test: no tier 3 free mod line is rarer than another line of the same item.
- **Why it hits experts.** The free clue becomes a lead rather than an answer. The expert pays for the centre with a Flare or for a rare mod with a Scroll. That also makes Flare placement a skill: knowing *where* an item's tell sits is knowledge.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** Nothing is hidden that a supply can't reveal. The rule only stops the free sample from being the best sample.

#### Tier 4: Unmarked

- **Rule.** After a miss, the board doesn't change. The wrong build is listed under the answer line so it can't be submitted twice, but no name is struck and no syllable greys out. A Name strike supply still strikes and greys out as usual. So does a Scroll of Wisdom under the Strike Through boon.
- **Data.** None needed.
- **Why it hits experts.** Today a confident wrong guess is half a clue: it costs life but clears names for the next try. Here it only costs life, so the expected value of answering on a hunch drops. That makes buying certainty *before* submitting the better move, and Name strikes become a real buy for experts too.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** Narrowing the board now comes from what you know or what you pay for, never from trial and error. That fits "every submit counts".

#### Tier 5: Lightless

- **Rule.** A zone's first item opens with its free clue. Its second and third items open with nothing. The zone's *rule* (Ritual's no-Lore, Ultimatum's 2x, and so on) still applies to all 3 items.
- **Data.** None needed.
- **Why it hits experts.** About 20 of the run's 33 items open blind. On a 6 to 8 name board, a blind guess costs 35 to 45 life. Even a top player now spends at least one supply on most items, so towns, gold and pack space matter all run. The Lamplighter boon (a free first patch) becomes a real pick at this tier.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The expert edge doesn't vanish; it moves. One Flare plus deep knowledge answers an item a newcomer would need four supplies for. The run turns into "how little can I buy and still be sure", which is a knowledge question.

Readability check: each card line has under 70 characters, no numbers to remember, and a verb that names the thing taken away.

Alternates kept on the bench, if one of the above fails a playtest:

- **Finer Veil.** "The Veil is cut 5 by 5; Flares still lift 2." Every patch shrinks from 1/16 of the art to 1/25. Simple, but it also taxes newcomers' Flares.
- **No Count.** "The answer line no longer shows how many words." Only works if the open question ends with tier 1 showing word counts.

### Per-zone and per-card ideas that make an expert *want* to spend

#### a) Unidentified (special card)

- **Card text.** "x3 reward. Opens without its free clue; a Scroll of Wisdom on it reveals the free clue too."
- **Odds.** One of the special cards. With five specials, that's about 1 card in 30.
- **Why an expert spends.**
  - At step 10, a right answer pays 7 x 3 = 21 gold against 7.
  - A blind guess on a 6-name board risks 35 life, and a miss halves the reward.
  - One 6-gold Scroll buys the zone's clue *and* a mod line. For an expert, that is usually enough, so the Scroll is the obvious buy, and it's a thematic one: in PoE a Scroll of Wisdom identifies items.
- **With the Clean Hands boon.** Clean Hands pays double for an item answered without supplies, so a blind answer here pays x6. The expert's gamble is still there; it just has a price.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The multiplier pays for *knowledge per supply*. Whoever needs only one clue earns the most.

#### b) Breach (zone type, found in the fog)

- **Free clue.** 1 patch.
- **Rule.** "Rewards climb x1, x2, x3 over the zone's items; a miss drops the climb to x1."
- **Numbers.** At step 8, with a base reward of 7 and a x1 card, the zone pays 7 + 14 + 21 = 42 gold against 21 in a Plain zone. A miss on the third item costs 14 gold on top of the life.
- **Why an expert spends.** The third item is worth three of the first, so even an 80% sure expert should Flare it. Supplies saved for the boss now compete with a sure 21 gold.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The decision is "am I sure enough for x3", and only knowledge answers that.

#### c) Betrayal (zone type, found in the fog)

- **Free clue.** "Two free patches: one from the answer, one from another name on the board. You aren't told which."
- **Rule.** Reward 1.5x.
- **Automation.** Both patches are cut on the host from art already in `public/items/`. The decoy is a random board name and is drawn from the same squares as tier 3, so it shows no tell of its own.
- **Why an expert spends.** Recognising both patches narrows the board to two names. Breaking that tie takes a Flare on the answer's art or a Scroll. A newcomer who recognises neither patch gets little from them.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The clue is honest about being half a lie. Turning it into a 50/50 *is* the knowledge, and the last step is paid for. The Syndicate league's theme of deception fits it.

All three fit the plan's slots for later ideas: "special cards are a good place to add new ideas later", and hidden zone types are unlocked from the fog.

### What to measure

The plan's first measure, "items answered without using a supply" (target: under half), should be logged **per tier and per player's Codex accuracy band**. The ladder works if experts' no-supply share falls tier by tier: roughly 70% at tier 1, 50% at 2, 35% at 3, 30% at 4 and 15% at 5. Their win rate shouldn't collapse along the way.

## 2. Losses: something small that survives

The problem: a loss at minute ~20 keeps only uncovered fog. A casual viewer on a phone sees 20 minutes turn into nothing and closes the tab after the second one.

### Option A: Waypoint (a gentler tier 1)

- **Rule (tier 1 only).** "Once per run, a death after the step 8 town returns you to the last town you passed, as you arrived there. The road after it rolls new items."
- **Effect.** A late loss costs about 8 minutes instead of 20. A tier 1 win after a Waypoint still unlocks tier 2.
- **For.** It hits the sunk-time pain exactly, and it fits PoE's waypoints and checkpoints.
- **Against.**
  - It makes tier 1 noticeably easier, so the "about half for regulars" target needs re-tuning.
  - A run can stretch past 30 minutes.
  - It is a rewind, which sits uneasily with "every submit counts".
  - Co-op needs the whole party rewound.
- **Cost, roughly 2 to 3 days.**
  - Copy the resume state on arriving at each town. The resume already saves after every decision, so that's an extra key.
  - Restore flow and tier 1 gate.
  - Co-op sync of the rewind.
  - Tests and a QA scene.
  - Tuning the tier 1 numbers again.

### Option B: The Grave (recommended)

- **Rule.**
  - "When a run is lost after the first town, the item that ended it is buried at your next camp with up to 3 of your unused supplies. An empty pack buries one Flare."
  - "Name it there: 4 names, 2 free patches, one try, no life at stake. Right: dig the supplies up. Wrong: the grave is gone."
  - "A grave lasts one camp; a new loss replaces it."
- **Why it works for casual viewers.**
  - The loss screen already reveals the killer's full art and name. The next run then *opens* with a chance to prove you learnt it, which is a near-certain, quick win in the first 30 seconds of run 2.
  - "Revenge on Ab Aeterno" is a sentence a streamer says out loud, and it gives the viewer a reason to tap "New run" right away. The Codex already rewards this exact moment with the Sweet Revenge achievement.
  - It's one tap-sized screen, the same shape as the Gambler and Strongbox, so it works fine on a phone.
- **Why wins stay meaningful.**
  - Tiers and fog boons still come only from wins.
  - The most a grave gives is 3 supplies, about 12 to 18 gold, against the 20 gold camp budget and the roughly 200 gold a run earns.
  - Nothing stacks.
  - The first-town requirement stops "die at step 1 to carry the camp shopping over".
- **Why it adds no grind.** One grave, one try, consumed at the next camp, and no meter fills. A player on a losing streak gets the same small thing each time, never more.
- **Why trivia stays the heart.** The carry-over is earned by knowing an item you just failed. It is a miniature Necropolis zone, and the name ties it to the Necropolis league's graveyards.
- **Co-op.** The host's grave, answered by the usual proposal and majority.
- **Cost, roughly 1 to 1.5 days** once events (Gambler and Strongbox) exist.
  - One record in `storage.ts`: `{itemId, supplies[], fromTier}` with parse and serialize, plus a test. About 40 lines.
  - One line on the loss screen: "Buried at your camp: Ab Aeterno, 2 Flares, 1 Scroll".
  - The camp reuses the event item screen (one try, no life).
  - The grave is consumed on submit so a reload can't replay it.
  - A QA scene, "camp with a grave".

### Option C: Keepsake

- **Rule.** "After a loss that reached the first town, your next camp offers one boon you held, to keep for that run." Boons found in the fog are excluded, so they still have to be brought home.
- **For.** Cheapest to build, and it carries the run's "build" feel across.
- **Against.**
  - It's power for nothing, unrelated to trivia.
  - A boon is worth about 40 gold at town prices, more than the Grave, so it narrows the gap between a loss and a win.
  - It blurs the "bring boons home by winning" rule.
- **Cost.** About 1 day: storage, one camp card pick, a test.

### Recommendation

**The Grave (B).**

- It is the only option where the carry-over comes from trivia and not from mercy.
- It turns the moment a casual viewer is most likely to quit into the hook for the next run.
- It's the cheapest of the three to tune: it doesn't touch the tier 1 win rate.

Keep the Waypoint (A) in reserve. If first-time players still win under about 25% of tier 1 runs once the Grave is in, add the Waypoint to tier 1 only. The two don't conflict.
